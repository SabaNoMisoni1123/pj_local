# Research Analysis Interview Design Workflow

## Purpose

Create interview or questionnaire designs that connect the research objective, hypotheses, target respondents, questions, and expected evidence.

## Source Material Handling

When source materials for the interview design are PDFs, Office files, email exports, or other non-text artifacts, use the markitdown-mcp server to convert supported files to Markdown when direct reading is impractical. Treat converted Markdown as a derived aid and flag questions or assumptions that depend on conversion-sensitive content such as tables, comments, charts, or attachments.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the research objective, decision context, target respondent type, interview format, time limit, and output format.
2. Identify what must be learned from the interview and what should be verified by other sources.
3. Translate issues and hypotheses into question blocks.
4. Order questions from low-burden background confirmation to core questions, then examples, evidence, constraints, and closing confirmation.
5. For each question, clarify the intent, expected evidence, follow-up probes, and whether it confirms a fact, opinion, experience, estimate, or recommendation.
6. Avoid leading questions, double-barreled questions, ambiguous terms, and questions that ask respondents to disclose inappropriate confidential information.
7. Include screening conditions, respondent attributes, consent or recording notes when relevant.
8. Prepare a short post-interview synthesis frame for findings, quotes or claims, confidence, and follow-up actions.

## Quality Criteria

- Tie every major question to a research issue or hypothesis.
- Mark questions that require careful wording due to confidentiality, sensitivity, or speculation.
- Separate factual confirmation, respondent views, and Codex/user interpretation.
- Include enough context for the interviewer, but do not overload the respondent with explanations.
- Do not invent respondent facts, affiliations, or prior answers.
